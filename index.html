The open self-improvement governance standard for AI agents.

Read the Spec · The Protocol · Evidence · Case Study: AGD · Contribute

What it is

wRS governs how agents change: their own prompts, instructions, and workflows. wOS governs how agents behave; wRS governs how they improve. Like wOS, it is a specification rather than a framework. It sits on top of your runtime and defines the contract a self-improving system must satisfy before it ships a change to itself.

The contract in one line: improvement is earned by pre-registered evidence, shipped through a narrow class, verified by a checker the actor cannot write, and reversible by a party other than the one that shipped.

Why it exists

Agents today improve in exactly two ways, and both are wrong:

  • Ungoverned. Self-rewriting loops that edit their own instructions continuously and keep whatever scores better. No gates, no audit, and in the wild examples they run generated code with production permissions. Fast and unauditable.
  • Not at all. Frozen prompts. Every improvement waits for a human to notice, design, test, and apply it. Safe and slow, and it never compounds.

wRS defines the middle path: a charter-gated improvement loop. An agent may self-initiate and auto-ship inside a pre-registered, mechanically verified class, while spend, external effects, irreversible actions, and boundary changes stay human. The level you operate at is explicit, printed, and checkable.

The doctrine is production-tested

wRS was authored inside wgnr.ai and validated the way it prescribes: pre-registered decision rules, blind runs, self-tested mechanical graders, and literal verdicts across four experiments, including a published null result where fleet telemetry did not confirm the laboratory finding. The full evidence, failures included, is in evidence/RESULTS.md.

Conformance levels

LevelDefinitionWho it is for
L0 ManualHumans design, run, judge, apply everything.Starting out
L1 Autonomous executionThe agent does the work; a human approves every change.Most production teams today
L2 Charter-gated autonomyThe agent self-initiates and auto-ships inside an allowlisted, mechanically verified class; spend, gates, and boundaries stay human; an independent verifier can roll back.Teams with evidence discipline
L3 Unconstrained self-modificationContinuous autonomous rewriting with no gates.Non-conformant by definition. Never.

Positioning

wRS does not compete with adjacent standards. Each governs a different layer of the same stack:

StandardWhat it governsRelationship
wOS (wgnr.ai)How agents behaveSibling: behavior vs. change
ACS (Microsoft)Security policyComplementary: policy vs. change process
AGENTS.md (AAIF)Context and instructionsComplementary: knowledge vs. change governance
Agent OS (Builder Methods)Coding standardsComplementary: code vs. self-change
